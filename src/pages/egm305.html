EGM305 The Atmosphere

The Practical Companion works through all three practicals of EGM305, a second-year module at Ulster University on weather, atmospheric circulation and climate, as one continuous book, with slides and an interactive atlas alongside. The module is led by Prof. Derek Jackson with Dr Ambrose McCarron; these materials are my companions to the practical sheets, written while demonstrating on the module.

The Practical Companion

One book in three parts, one part per practical, in the order of the module: Practical 1 describes the structure of the atmosphere, Practical 2 explains how the Sun heats it, and Practical 3 how that uneven heating fills it with moisture and sets it in motion. Chapters, equations, figures and tables are numbered continuously, the three parts refer to each other wherever one practical builds on another, and there is one set of conventions, one constants table and one reference list.

How to use it

Every chapter follows the same order. The ideas come first, in plain language; every important equation is numbered and followed by a list of what each symbol means, its unit and the value used; each question of the sheet appears in a box, is solved one step per line with every number shown, and is answered in a green box followed by a check. Red boxes point out the few slips in the printed answer keys; orange boxes answer the questions students most often ask; each chapter ends with a practice set and its answers.

Try each question on paper first. Cover the answer box, work it through, then compare. If your answer differs, find the step where your reasoning diverged before reading on; that step is where the learning is. The PDFs are fully hyperlinked, so every equation, figure, table, section and reference can be clicked.

Atmosphere Atlas

A single, self-contained web page: an interactive globe, which flattens to a map, that explains the weather, the oceans and the climate from first principles to graduate level. Every layer is drawn from real data (NOAA, NASA, IBTrACS, Natural Earth and others) and the page works offline once loaded.

  • A 4D globe. Rotate, zoom and move through the seasons and the hours of the day; layers for temperature, pressure, winds at four heights, the jet stream, vertical motion, water vapour, rain, cloud, sea-surface temperature, ocean currents, sea ice and climate zones.
  • 125 topics in 13 chapters, each at three levels (simple, in depth, expert with equations): the Sun and seasons, why the wind blows, the global circulation, the ocean, weather systems, tropical cyclones, ENSO and other climate modes, water, climate change, the sky, and forecasting.
  • Laboratories for the Coriolis effect, balance of forces, rising air, the Ekman spiral, Rossby waves, monsoons, depressions, storm tracks, El Niño, the energy balance, Milankovitch cycles and chaos, plus the Practical 3 laboratories.
  • Real charts with data-table twins, seven guided tours, a glossary of 217 terms, light and dark themes, unit switching and a phone layout.

The atlas is about 11 MB, because the data travel inside the page, so it can take a few seconds to open on a slow connection. A browser with WebGL is needed.

Reading

The module reading schedule follows two textbooks: Lutgens, Tarbuck and Herman, The Atmosphere: An Introduction to Meteorology, and Ahrens and Henson, Meteorology Today; each part of the companion names the chapters to read. Free, open alternatives:

  • Stull, R. (2017) Practical Meteorology: An Algebra-based Survey of Atmospheric Science. University of British Columbia. Free online edition.
  • The Met Office: Learn about weather pages, and its surface pressure charts for practising map analysis on today's weather.

Corrections

Every answer in the companion was recomputed from the data on the sheets and checked against the printed answer keys. The few places where a key needs correcting are explained where they arise and collected in each part's appendix (Practical 1: § A.4; Practical 2: § B.4; Practical 3: § C.6).

If you find a mistake in any of these materials, or a step that is not clear, please tell me in the lab or by email. Corrections are made in the source and a new version is posted here.
