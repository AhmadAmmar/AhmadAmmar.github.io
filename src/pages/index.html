Ahmad Ammar

Geocomputation, Earth observation and urban informatics

I am a doctoral researcher in the School of Geography and Environmental Sciences at Ulster University, Coleraine, funded by a Vice-Chancellor’s Research Scholarship (VCRS). My project asks whether open satellite imagery and geospatial data can estimate the energy efficiency of buildings (the A–G bands of Energy Performance Certificates) reliably enough to be used across whole cities, across time, and in places where certificates are scarce. Greater London is the data-rich development area and Belfast the transfer test. More on the research →

I trained in geo-informatics (BS, gold medal) and in remote sensing and geo-information science (MS), and worked on crime and election mapping at the Punjab Information Technology Board, weather-routing data pipelines at Offshore Monitoring Ltd, and tax-compliance research at the Mahbub ul Haq Research Centre, LUMS. Earlier research covered mangrove canopy height and biomass from GEDI LiDAR in the Indus Delta and deep-learning building extraction from fused satellite and aerial imagery.

At Ulster I work as a postgraduate teaching assistant across physical geography, environmental science and GIS modules, and I am completing the Advance HE-accredited First Steps to Supporting Learning and Teaching (FST) programme. For EGM305 The Atmosphere I write open study guides and an interactive atlas of the weather and climate. More on teaching →

Outside the lab I write on cities, climate, water and political economy in South Asia, on Substack and in the pamphlet Marxian Economics 101. More writing →
