38.2 Mistakes to avoid¶

  • Using \(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)in \(\sigma T^4\), Wien’s law, the ideal gas law or the parcel buoyancy formula: these need kelvin.

  • Saying “heat” when you mean “temperature”: temperature is a state, heat is energy moving because of a temperature difference.

  • Thinking metal is colder than wood at the same room temperature: it only feels colder.

  • Forgetting that the cup and the container must hold equal volumes in Activity 1.2, or not stirring.

  • Calculating the Archimedes ballast with the ball’s mass in place of its volume, or forgetting the stopper above the surface.

  • Writing the prediction after the experiment. Predict first, then test; explain the difference.

38.3 Self-test¶

  1. Air at 20\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)and 101 325 Pa: what is its density? [1.204 kg m\(^{-3}\)]

  2. How many joules melt 25 g of ice at 0\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)? [8350 J]

  3. 200 g of water at 60\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)is mixed with 100 g at 15\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\). Final temperature? [45\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)]

  4. What extra pressure does a diver feel 25 m below the sea surface? [\(\approx 2.5\times10^5\) Pa, 2.5 atm]

  5. A balloon of 3.0 L at 1 atm is taken to 0.6 atm at the same temperature. New volume? [5.0 L]

  6. A ball of volume 200 cm\(^3\) and mass 50 g: how much water ballast for neutral buoyancy? [\(\approx\) 150 mL]

  7. Why does a coastal town have milder winters than an inland one at the same latitude? [the ocean’s large heat capacity]

  8. An air parcel is 2\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)colder than its surroundings at 280 K. Which way does it accelerate, and how fast? [down, 0.07 m s\(^{-2}\)]
