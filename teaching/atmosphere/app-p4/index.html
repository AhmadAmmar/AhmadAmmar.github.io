D.3 Corrections and clarifications to the handbook¶

The handbook (EGM305 module team n.d.) is sound; these points are worth knowing when you read it beside this part.

  1. Kelvin. It gives \(K = \ensuremath{^{\circ}\mathrm{C}}+ 273.16\) and calls 0\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)the “freezing (or triple) point”. The conversion is \(+273.15\); 273.16 K (0.01\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)) is water’s triple point. The 0.01 never matters at lab precision.

  2. Stefan–Boltzmann constant. It gives \(5.7\times10^{-8}\); the value is \(5.670\times10^{-8}\) \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\) K\(^{-4}\).

  3. “About 4000 times”. Per volume, water needs about 3400 times more heat than air to warm by 1\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)(\(4.18\times10^6\) against \(1.23\times10^3\) J m\(^{-3}\) K\(^{-1}\), § 33.2); “thousands of times” is the safe statement.

  4. Latent heat of vaporisation. 2260 J g\(^{-1}\) is the value at 100\(^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\); at ocean-surface temperatures it is about 2450 J g\(^{-1}\), slightly larger.

  5. Pressure halving height. “About 5.6 km” corresponds to a scale height of about 8 km; with the real, colder upper air the halving height is 5 to 6 km (§ 34.2).

  6. Activity 1.1C materials: “Matched” should read “Matches”.
